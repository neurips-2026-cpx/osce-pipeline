## Common diseases that present with cough

- Pneumonia: typically presents with productive cough, fever, dyspnea,
  pleuritic chest pain. Examination may reveal crackles. CXR shows
  consolidation.
- Acute bronchitis: cough lasting fewer than three weeks, often viral,
  may follow upper respiratory infection. No focal consolidation.
- Asthma: episodic cough often worse at night or after exertion. Wheezing
  on exam. Reversible airflow obstruction.
- COPD: chronic productive cough on most days for at least three months
  in two consecutive years. History of smoking is common.
- Postnasal drip: cough triggered by upper-airway secretions, often with
  rhinitis or sinusitis.
- GERD: chronic dry cough worse when supine, may be associated with
  heartburn or regurgitation.
- Tuberculosis: chronic cough greater than three weeks, weight loss,
  night sweats, hemoptysis in advanced cases.
- Lung cancer: persistent cough, hemoptysis, weight loss, fatigue,
  particularly in smokers older than 50.

## Key history-taking points

1. Onset and duration (acute < 3 weeks, subacute 3–8 weeks, chronic > 8).
2. Productive vs dry; sputum colour, volume, blood.
3. Associated symptoms: fever, dyspnea, chest pain, weight loss.
4. Triggers: cold air, exertion, lying down, allergens.
5. Exposures: tobacco, occupational dusts, sick contacts, travel.
6. Past medical history: asthma, COPD, GERD, malignancy, immune status.
7. Medications: ACE inhibitors (cough side effect), inhalers.

## Patient education

- Explain likely differential and reassure when appropriate.
- Smoking cessation counselling when relevant.
- Indications for further imaging or referral (red flags: hemoptysis,
  weight loss, persistent symptoms, age > 50 with smoking history).
